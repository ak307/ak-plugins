# Visual design directions

Pick the best fit for the product (shortlist two or three, compare, choose),
then make it the product's own. These describe a character and its
tendencies — not a layout to copy.

| Direction | Fits | Character and tendencies | Risks to manage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Premium minimalist** | premium consumer products, focused tools, brands that sell quality | generous whitespace, very few elements, refined type, restrained palette, precise detail, quiet motion | emptiness without hierarchy; too little information for working users |
| **Modern editorial** | content-led sites, reports, corporate stories, knowledge products | strong typographic hierarchy, editorial grids, serif/sans pairings, large imagery, reading rhythm | slow for task-heavy apps; long line lengths |
| **Enterprise professional** | internal tools, admin, finance, HR, operations | clarity, density done well, a palette with character and strong semantic colours, consistent components, efficient navigation, a distinctive type choice and header | blandness (the default-sans, white-and-grey admin look); tiny type; grey-on-grey |
| **Modern SaaS** | B2B products, dashboards, onboarding-heavy apps | crisp components, clear navigation, product screenshots as heroes, purposeful accent colour, polished states | the generic SaaS card kit; looking like every competitor |
| **AI-native** | AI assistants, agent platforms, AI analytics | conversation and results as the centre, clear AI states (thinking, tool use, streaming), trust cues (sources, confirmations), calm surfaces with one expressive signal | gimmicky glows and gradients; fake "thinking"; hiding what the AI did |
| **Futuristic technology** | deep-tech, infrastructure, developer and data platforms | dark or high-contrast surfaces, technical type (mono accents), precise lines, data visualisation, controlled light | unreadable contrast; decoration over function |
| **Creative expressive** | campaigns, cultural, youth or creative brands | bold colour, unusual layouts, illustration, playful type, expressive motion | usability and accessibility sacrificed for effect |
| **Luxury** | high-end property, hospitality, premium services | restraint, elegant serif or fine sans, rich neutrals, slow considered motion, photography | slow pages; low contrast; vague calls to action |
| **Swiss-inspired** | institutions, transport, public information, design-literate brands | strict grid, strong sans type, asymmetric balance, flat colour, clear hierarchy | coldness; over-rigid layouts on small screens |
| **Product-focused** | hardware, apps and services sold on what they do | the product (screens, objects) as the hero, clear benefits, demonstrations, motion that shows how it works | marketing fluff; heavy media |
| **Data-centric** | analytics, monitoring, operations control rooms, KPI dashboards | information hierarchy first, charts and tables as the design, tabular figures, semantic colour, live-update motion | chart junk; colour overload; everything shouting |
| **Consumer-friendly** | public-facing apps, residents, customers, broad audiences | warmth, plain language, large touch targets, friendly illustration, forgiving flows | childishness; inconsistent playfulness |
| **Brutalist** | creative studios, statements, niche audiences that value rawness | raw structure, default-looking elements used deliberately, heavy type, stark contrast | hostility to users; accessibility gaps |
| **Neo-minimalist** | modern tools and brands that want calm with character | minimal structure plus one or two distinctive moves (a colour field, a type treatment, a material), soft depth | sameness with other "clean" products |

## Choosing

1. From the evaluation (brand, industry, audience, category, density,
   expectations, workflows, hierarchy, accessibility, platform), shortlist
   the directions that fit.
2. For each: one sentence on how it would serve this product, its main risk
   here.
3. Choose the best fit (a primary direction with at most one supporting
   influence). Record the choice and the alternatives in `DESIGN.md`.

## Making it this product's own

Within the direction, derive from the product's own world:

- **Palette** — roles first (background, surfaces, ink, primary, accent,
  semantic), values chosen for the product, contrast checked.
- **Type** — one or two families that suit the direction and the languages
  used; a deliberate scale.
- **Layout idea** — how the primary job is composed on the main screen.
- **Shape language** — radii, borders, depth.
- **Signature idea** — one memorable idea for the product, drawn from its
  content or domain, shown on the screens where it helps.
- **Voice** — how it speaks.

Business apps still need clarity first: whatever the direction, the primary
job stays effortless and the quality floor applies.
