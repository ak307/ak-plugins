# Premium UI/UX design standard — the most important rule

AK's standard for every UI task. It overrides "it works": a design or
interaction is only finished when it also feels exceptional.

## The standard

Every page, screen, and interaction must be evaluated against the highest
standards of premium product design.

For every page you visit, every button you click, every tab you navigate,
every dropdown you open, and every action you perform, continuously ask
yourself:

> **Does this feel premium, polished, beautiful, intuitive, and impressive —
> or does it feel boring, basic, generic, or unfinished?**

If anything feels ordinary, unpolished, underwhelming, or below the expected
standard, improve it. Do not settle for mediocre design. The expectation is
a top-tier, Apple-level user experience where every detail feels
intentional, refined, seamless, and exceptionally well-crafted.

It applies to how the whole product **behaves**, not only how it looks:

| Area | Must feel |
|---|---|
| **Visual design** — layout, typography, spacing, colours, hierarchy, animations, visual consistency | intentional, refined, consistent |
| **Buttons and interactions** | every click responsive, satisfying, intuitive and polished |
| **Navigation** — tabs, menus, dropdowns, sidebars, transitions | seamless and effortless |
| **Loading states** | elegant, purposeful transitions and indicators; the product never feels frozen or unfinished |
| **Downloading and processing** | every progress indicator, confirmation and completion state thoughtfully designed |
| **Feedback and micro-interactions** — hover, pressed, tooltips, notifications, success and error states | refined |
| **Transitions and animations** | smooth, natural, subtle and purposeful — never distracting or excessive |
| **Responsiveness** | every interaction equally polished on every device and screen size |

## Non-negotiable principle

Never accept a design or interaction simply because it works. It must also
feel exceptional. Continuously evaluate, refine, and improve every
experience until it meets the standard of a world-class premium product.

The goal is not merely a functional interface. It is an experience that
makes users think: *"This is beautiful. This feels incredibly smooth. Every
detail has been thoughtfully designed. This feels like a truly premium
product."* Treat every screen, every interaction, and every transition as an
opportunity to deliver that.

## How to apply it

- **While designing and building:** hold every decision to this bar —
  hierarchy, type, spacing, colour, every state of every control, every
  transition — not only the hero screen.
- **While verifying (`arkar-ui-visual-qa`):** do the premium pass — walk
  every page, button, tab, dropdown, menu, form, loading, processing,
  download, success and error state you built or changed; ask the question
  above for each; note anything basic, generic or unfinished; refine it;
  re-check. Repeat until nothing in scope feels ordinary.
- **Scope:** everything you build or change must meet the standard before
  you report it done. Ordinary-feeling screens or interactions you notice
  outside the request go to AK as labelled premium-upgrade proposals (what
  feels basic, what you'd do) — the approval rule still applies.
- **Premium is rich, not plain.** Restraint means precision, never
  emptiness: crafted type, layered surfaces and depth, real imagery or
  product visuals, polished components in every state, motion on every
  state change (`arkar-ui-art-direction/references/premium-craft.md`). Lazy
  effects (stock gradients, glows, random shadows) are still out; crafted
  ones are in.
- **Refine in rounds.** First build → screenshots → judge against the
  standard and the best product in the category → list the visible
  shortfalls → fix → repeat. New products and redesigns get at least two
  rounds; stop only when nothing feels ordinary.
- **Honesty:** in the final message, say what the premium pass covered and
  what you refined; never claim polish you didn't verify.
